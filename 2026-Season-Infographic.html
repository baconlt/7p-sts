05

Sunshine predicts rescues. Surf doesn't.

10×

A sunny shift is ten times likelier to produce a rescue than a cloudy one, while calm-to-rough surf moves the rate less than 12%. 139 of the season's 189 rescues happened on sunny cards. The ocean isn't what makes the day dangerous — the crowd is.

The 85° rule

One number for a staffing trigger

Under 85°F the crew averaged 0.12 rescues and 14.2 preventives per card. At 85° and above: 0.35 rescues and 25.9 preventives — 2.9× the rescue rate and 1.8× the preventive load.

It's a medical threshold too. All four heat-emergency incidents this season occurred at 85°F or hotter — 85°, 89°, 95° and 102°. Three of the four were on July 4. Hottest card of the year: 105°F. Season mean: 80.8°F.

Modal clock-in is 8:45:00 sharp (817 punches), with only a thin late tail. The soft spot is the other end of the day — 118 records auto-closed because nobody punched out, driving most of the 1,057 after-the-fact payroll edits. A 5:00 PM clock-out nudge would fix most of it.
Method — Straight counts off the live STS workbook; no modeling. Nine duplicate stat cards removed (raw: 1,056 cards / 17,604 preventives / 192 rescues). August is partial. Stat cards are self-reported at a 77% filing rate — ratios are sound, absolute totals are a floor. Rescue rates are per card, not per day, since staffing changed sharply. Temp bands are lower-inclusive. Punctuality covers 7–10 AM clock-ins only. Voting tallies omitted — ballots are anonymous, results held for the banquet.
